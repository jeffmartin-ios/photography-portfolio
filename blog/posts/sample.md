Dotonbori is less a place and more a full-sensory experience. From the moment you step off the subway and into the throng of people, you're hit with a wave of sound, light, and, most importantly, the smell of incredible street food. My goal for the day was to capture not just the sights, but the feeling of being swept up in this vibrant river of humanity.

### The Sea of Neon

The first thing that strikes you is the sheer scale of the signage. Giant mechanical crabs, dragons, and the iconic Glico Running Man loom over the canal, their reflections dancing on the water's surface. Photographing here at night is a challenge; the dynamic range is extreme, with deep shadows punctuated by blindingly bright neon.

> My approach was to embrace the chaos. Instead of trying to find a clean shot, I focused on layering elements—the crowds, the signs, the steam rising from a takoyaki stand—to create a sense of depth and energy.

I spent hours just walking, finding different angles. A low-angle shot can make the signs feel even more monumental, while a long exposure from one of the bridges can turn the passing boats into streaks of light.

The gallery above showcases some of my favorite shots from the day, both in the main thoroughfare and in the quieter, more intimate alleyways that branch off it. Each photo tells a small part of the story of this incredible place.

Capturing Dotonbori was a reminder that sometimes the best street photography isn't about isolating a single subject, but about capturing the spirit of the entire scene.
